These would be called transactional databases, Oracle, Microsoft SQL servers. And then you'd have these analytical databases that would get these overnight dumps of data, where they would compute more sophisticated to produce reports, charts, Power BI tools. Materialize is a database built from the ground up to be able to ingest changes to the data set millisecond by millisecond at extremely high volume and keep answers updated ahead of time so that you don't have to essentially wait till tomorrow morning to get the refresh of the new data points incorporated. There's obvious applications to this. A great example is probably the finance application, sort of obvious, but it's much beyond that, right? Anybody who fundamentally has a business process that is gated on updating views of the latest pieces of from supply chain to logistics to advertising to personalization, all of these different industries really do benefit from operating on the latest and greatest piece of information rather than trying to work off of yesterday's information or spend a lot of effort predicting data that the left hand of the organization already knows, but it hasn't gotten around to the right hand yet. So right now it has to expend immense amounts of effort to sort of predict things that they could just stream from other parts of the organization if they had the capability to do so.
