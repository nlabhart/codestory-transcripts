Reclaim Protocol is essentially a way to verify information about a user in a way that is resistant to AI fraud and is privacy preserving. There are three verticals in which we do most of our business. First is student verification. Are you a student or not? This is largely in the context of student discounts. If there are companies that want to do student discounts, they do student verification. The second vertical is employment verification. Now, where do you work? and this is being used by companies to give out loans and also in the hiring. So let's say, Noah, if I had to verify, if you claim that you study at Stanford, how can I be sure over the interview? So one way that you could convince me is by saying, hey, look, I have my student ID. Here's a photograph. And you send that across to me. And this is what most of the verification companies today do. Maybe it's not a person on the other side, but it might be a software that is trying to do this comparison. And essentially what they're looking for is visual cues. cue of, hey, does this look like a Stanford ID? And they're looking for visual cues of, has this data been morphed? Has this image been Photoshopped in some form? What is obvious right now is all of these companies that are doing verification using this visual cue verification mechanism are being bombarded with AI-generated documents. That's where Reclaim Protocol comes in. We say, hey, truth should not be grounded in visual authenticity. Rather, it should be grounded in cryptographic and mathematical proofs. So what we say is, hey, if you're a student at Stanford, you should have access to your Stanford student portal. Why don't you log in over there? And once you log in, you should see the text that says, yeah, Noah is a registered student at Stanford. Whatever shows up on that user profile. And we generate a cryptographic proof that all of this happened. that you open stanford.edu, you logged in over there, and on the page after login you see the text that says you are a student at Stanford. All of this could be packaged in proof called a zero knowledge proof and we are very lucky that we are at a time where the verification as a problem became super big because of AI and right about the same time the technology called zero-knowledge proofs also matured, which allowed us to solve this problem in the way that I just mentioned. That is, can I ask you to log into a website and prove to me what information you're seeing on your screen in a way that is tamper-resistant and still privacy-preserving?
